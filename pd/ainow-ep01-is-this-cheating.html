What's happening on campuses right now

AI agents can now complete entire online courses on a student's behalf, well beyond copy-pasted essays.

Online courses, where students may never meet instructors or peers, are especially exposed.

False alarms

AI detectors remain unreliable and have falsely accused many students, especially non-native English speakers.

An accusation built on a detector score can damage a student who did nothing wrong.

Takeaways

Three things to remember

Cheating is now a spectrum.

It is defined by each course's rules and the purpose of the task, not by the tool itself. Clear, explicit policies matter.

Detection alone is not evidence.

Detectors misfire, and the harm lands on innocent students, often multilingual learners.

The lasting fix is assessment design.

Process, conversation, and transparency make learning visible in ways surveillance cannot.
