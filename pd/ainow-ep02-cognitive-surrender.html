The news in 3 minutes

What the research is showing in 2026

MIT, Aug 2026Campus
"Cognitive surrender"

An MIT committee warns that getting answers from a bot creates an "illusion of learning" for students who reach for AI at the first hint of struggle.

The report also found students skipping office hours and communal study. In a spring 2026 campus survey, 46% of undergraduates and 60% of graduate students reported frequent generative AI use.
UC Irvine, Jun 2026Math
Faster, not smarter

Time spent on math word problems fell 31% for high school and 27% for college students after ChatGPT arrived, while proctored exam performance on those problems dropped.

Graphing problems, which are harder to hand to AI, did not decline. That points to AI use rather than general learning loss.
China, Jun 2026Long-term
27,000 students

A 30-month study of secondary students found assigned work got better while thinking got worse.

The loss stayed hidden until the moment the tool could not be used, such as an exam.
2026 reportingThe other side
Design matters

Carefully designed AI tutors have improved achievement in controlled experiments by asking questions instead of giving answers.

Passive use can lead to skill decay, but structured, deliberate use may actually support critical thinking and creativity.

Big idea

Offloading is normal. Surrender is different.

We have always handed thinking to tools. The question is what we stop doing when we do.

Level 1

Healthy offloading

The tool handles something you already understand, so you can focus on harder thinking.

Example: using a calculator for arithmetic while you reason through a physics problem.
Level 2

Skipping the practice

The tool does the exact work the assignment was designed to build. You finish faster but practice less.

Example: having AI solve the word problems in a homework set meant to build problem-solving.
Level 3

Cognitive surrender

You stop judging. The AI's answer replaces your own reasoning, even when it is wrong.

Example: submitting an AI answer you cannot explain, and not noticing it contains an error.

Takeaways

Three things to remember

Faster is not the same as learning.

The struggle AI removes is often the exact place learning happens. Better homework can hide weaker thinking.

How you use it matters more than whether.

An AI that gives answers and an AI that asks questions lead to very different outcomes.

Protect the skills you'll need without the tool.

Try first, then use AI to check, question, and push your thinking further.
