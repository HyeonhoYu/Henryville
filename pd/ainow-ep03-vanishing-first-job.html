The news in 3 minutes

What's happening to first jobs in 2026

Stanford, Aug 2026Fewer hires
A widening gap

Young workers (22 to 25) in highly AI-exposed jobs are falling further behind peers in less-exposed jobs. Experienced workers show no comparable gap.

The change is happening mainly through fewer new hires rather than layoffs. Declines are concentrated where AI substitutes for human tasks; where AI complements workers, employment is flat or rising.
PwC, Jun 2026Seniorization
7x

AI-exposed entry-level roles are seven times more likely to require "senior" skills like judgement and leadership.

These seniorized entry-level roles grew 35% since 2019, while other entry-level roles shrank 10%. Based on 2.4 million U.S. entry-level postings.
IBM, Feb 2026Counter-move
Triple

IBM plans to triple U.S. entry-level hiring in 2026, while rewriting what those jobs involve.

Its HR chief says AI can now do most entry-level jobs from two to three years ago, so junior developers spend less time coding and more time with clients. Cutting junior hiring, IBM argues, leaves no one ready for senior roles later.
CESifo / EPI, Oct 2026The other side
Not (yet) a collapse

Researchers found no clear rise in unemployment among new bachelor's graduates in summer 2026 compared with recent years.

Young workers without degrees have also seen rising unemployment, which suggests AI is not the only force making the market tough. Recent-grad unemployment was 5.6% in March 2026 (New York Fed).

Big idea

Three ways AI changes a first job

The same technology can shrink one entry-level job and grow another.

Substitute

AI does the junior task

When AI can do most of what a newcomer did, companies hire fewer newcomers.

Example: early-career customer service and software jobs saw some of the steepest declines for young workers.
Seniorize

The bar moves up

The job still exists, but now asks beginners for judgement, leadership, and people skills.

Example: an entry-level analyst posting that now expects client meetings and decision-making on day one.
Complement

AI frees up time

AI handles routine work, and people spend more time on the parts only they can do.

Example: teachers who use AI weekly report saving about 5.9 hours a week and reinvesting it in feedback and students.

The big question: if AI takes the junior tasks, where will future experts learn their craft?

Takeaways

Three things to remember

The ladder is changing, not just shrinking.

Hiring is falling where AI substitutes for junior work, while entry-level roles that demand judgement and people skills are growing.

Read the headlines carefully.

The evidence is real but mixed. A gap in AI-exposed jobs is not the same as a collapse of all graduate jobs.

Build what AI can't easily replace.

Judgement, relationships, leadership, and knowing how to work with AI. College is the place to practice them.
