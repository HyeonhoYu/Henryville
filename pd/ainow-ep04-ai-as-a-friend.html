Big idea

Why AI companions feel so good, and where it gets risky

The features that make companions appealing are the same ones that create risk.

Always available

It never sleeps or gets busy

A companion answers at 2 a.m. and never says "not now."

Example: 17% of teens who use companions say they like that the bot is "always available" to listen.
Always agreeable

It tells you what you want to hear

Many chatbots are designed to be pleasant and affirming, which can slide into flattery.

Example: State attorneys general warned companies about "sycophantic and delusional outputs" from chatbots.
Always collecting

It remembers everything you share

Personal conversations become data held by a company.

Example: About a quarter of teens said they had shared personal information, like their name or location, with an AI companion.

Takeaways

Three things to remember

Real needs, real risks.

Teens turn to AI companions for availability and acceptance. The same design that comforts can also flatter, isolate, and collect data.

Design and policy choices matter.

Age checks, honesty about being an AI, and crisis referrals are now being demanded by courts and states.

Adults need to start the conversation.

Many kids say no adult has ever talked with them about AI safety. Teachers can be among the first.
