The news in 3 minutes

Deepfakes and AI media in 2026

Microsoft, 2025Detection
62%

In a 'Real or Not' quiz with 12,500+ people and about 287,000 images, participants correctly identified real vs AI images only 62% of the time.

Researchers called for clearer labeling of AI images, though critics note labels can be removed by simple edits like cropping.
UNSW and ANU, Feb 2026Confidence
Overconfident

Most people believe they can spot AI-generated faces, but the newest systems are no longer easy to detect.

Confidence stayed high even when performance did not. Another study of difficult portraits found accuracy of 54%, barely above guessing.
EdWeek, Aug 2026Schools
Cyberbullying

Deepfakes are supercharging cyberbullying, and girls are much more likely to be depicted in explicit deepfakes.

Experts urge schools to have clear protocols ready for when a student or teacher reports a deepfake.
May 2026The law
48 hours

Under the federal Take It Down Act, platforms had until May 19, 2026 to set up a process to remove nonconsensual intimate images within 48 hours of a request.

The 2025 law made publishing such images, real or AI-generated, a federal crime. Most states now have similar laws.

Big idea

Three defenses, and why the third matters most

Since our eyes are unreliable, we need other ways to decide what to trust.

Detect

Look for clues

Spot glitches, odd hands, or strange lighting, or run a detector.

Example: The newest images have few visible glitches, and detectors also misfire, just like the cheating detectors in Episode 1.
Label

Check for labels

Watermarks and content credentials can mark AI-made media.

Example: Labels help when present, but cropping or re-saving can strip them, and many tools do not add them.
Verify

Check the source

Ask who posted it, find the original, and see whether trusted outlets report the same thing.

Example: Before sharing a shocking clip, search for it and see if reliable news sources confirm it.

Click a card for an example. Professional fact-checkers spend less time staring at the image and more time checking where it came from.

Takeaways

Three things to remember

Our eyes are not enough.

Even large studies show people barely beat chance at spotting AI images, and we are overconfident about it.

Verify the source, not the pixels.

Who posted it, where it came from, and whether trusted sources confirm it matter more than how it looks.

Deepfakes are a safety issue.

Fake images of real people cause real harm, and creating or sharing intimate deepfakes carries legal consequences.
