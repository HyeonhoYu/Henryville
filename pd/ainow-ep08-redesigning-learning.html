How schools are redesigning learning in 2026

2026Oral exams
Talk it through

A growing number of professors are turning to oral exams, and some universities now run faculty workshops on them.

One NYU professor used an AI agent to run oral exams at scale, examining 36 students in nine days, with AI-assisted grading reviewed across multiple models.
MIT, Aug 2026Mindset
"AI-aware"

MIT's committee recommends AI-aware approaches to teaching and assessment instead of trying to keep AI out of classrooms.

It calls on universities to rethink learning goals, assessment practices, and the role of in-person experiences.
2026K-12 policy
37 states

At least 37 states have published some form of AI guidance for K-12 schools. Ohio required every district to adopt an AI policy by July 1, 2026.

FutureEd tracked 71 bills across 27 states on AI in classroom instruction during the 2026 session.
Gallup, May 2026The gap
18%

Only 18% of teachers said they had received any formal guidance from their schools on how AI tools should be used.

State guidance is often not binding, and it may encourage AI literacy without saying what that is or how to teach it.

Big idea

Three shifts in assessment

Across this series, the same lesson kept coming back: change what we assess, not just how we police it.

From product

to process

Value drafts, version history, and thinking steps, not only the final product.

Example: Students submit a research log and two drafts along with the final essay.
From detection

to conversation

Replace detector scores with short conversations about the work.

Example: A five-minute oral check-in where students explain one key decision in their project.
From banning

to designing

Design tasks where AI use is visible and useful, or deliberately absent.

Example: Students critique an AI-generated lesson plan and improve it, citing how they used AI.

Takeaways

Three things to remember

Assessment has to change.

Process, conversation, and design make learning visible in ways detection never could.

AI literacy is part of teaching now.

Students need to learn when to use AI, when not to, and how to check it. So do teachers.

Teachers shape the culture.

With little formal guidance, the norms students live by will be set in classrooms like yours.

Thank you for thinking together.
