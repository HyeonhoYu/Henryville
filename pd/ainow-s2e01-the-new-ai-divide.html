The news in 3 minutes

The AI divide in 2026

U. of Chicago, 2026A new divide
Guidance gap

A survey of about 1,200 principals found unequal development of AI policies, training, and guidance across schools, not just unequal access to tools.

Schools serving more economically disadvantaged students showed systematically lower levels of AI integration.
College Board, Dec 2025School type
2x

Private schools are more than twice as likely as public schools to both permit AI use and have policies governing it.

Among public schools, those serving more students eligible for free or reduced-price lunch were least likely to have such policies.
USC CARE, 2025Family income
Unequal use

Wealthier teens have more exposure to AI and use it more often.

Over 40% of the highest-income parents said their teens had used AI for school, compared with far fewer of the lowest-income families.
2026The other side
Capacity matters

Researchers argue that whether AI narrows or widens gaps may depend less on access than on which schools build the capacity to guide its use.

That means teacher training and clear guidance can matter as much as buying tools.

Big idea

Three layers of the AI divide

Closing the gap takes more than handing out accounts.

Layer 1

Access

Devices, internet, and paid AI tools that free versions cannot match.

Example: A student with only a shared family phone and a free plan versus a student with a laptop and a paid AI subscription.
Layer 2

Guidance

School policies, teacher training, and clear expectations.

Example: Two schools both allow AI, but only one trains teachers and explains the rules to families.
Layer 3

Skills

Knowing how to use AI well, check its work, and decide when not to use it.

Example: AI literacy lessons can be taught "unplugged," with no devices at all.

Takeaways

Three things to remember

The divide has moved.

It is less about devices and more about policies, training, and guidance.

Gaps follow income and school type.

Students in high-poverty schools are least likely to get clear AI guidance.

Teachers can close part of the gap.

AI literacy does not require expensive tools, and it starts with you.
