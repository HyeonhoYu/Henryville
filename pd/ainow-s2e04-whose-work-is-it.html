The news in 3 minutes

AI and copyright in 2026

2025Settlement
$1.5 billion

Authors reached a $1.5 billion class action settlement with Anthropic, the largest known copyright payout in U.S. history.

Earlier, a judge had called training on legally acquired books "quintessentially transformative" fair use; the case continued over copies obtained from pirated sources.
Oct 2026Appeals court
Not fair use

A U.S. appeals court upheld a ruling that Ross Intelligence's training of its legal AI on Thomson Reuters material was not fair use.

Courts are reaching different results depending on the facts, especially whether the AI competes with the original.
Mar 2026Supreme Court
Humans only

The Supreme Court declined to hear Thaler v. Perlmutter, leaving in place the rule that works created entirely by AI cannot be copyrighted.

Applicants must disclose AI-generated portions; human-made changes to AI output can still be protected.
2025 to 2026Licensing
Opt-in deals

Some music companies settled with AI music firms through licensing deals that let artists opt in.

Supporters see licensing as a path forward; critics worry most creators lack the power to negotiate.

Big idea

Three questions courts are asking

Most AI copyright fights come down to one of these.

Input

Can AI train on it?

Is copying works to train AI a "fair use," and does it matter how the copies were obtained?

Example: Rulings have gone different ways depending on the facts and whether the AI competes with the original.
Output

Does it copy?

Does what the AI produces reproduce or compete with a creator's work?

Example: An image that closely imitates a living artist's signature style raises harder questions than a generic picture.
Authorship

Who owns it?

Only human-created work can be copyrighted. How much human contribution is enough?

Example: A student who heavily revises and arranges AI drafts adds human authorship; a raw AI output has none.

Takeaways

Three things to remember

The law is still being written.

Courts are deciding case by case, and results vary.

Human authorship still matters.

Work made entirely by AI cannot be copyrighted in the U.S.

Teach attribution and respect.

Crediting AI help, and respecting creators, is part of academic integrity.
