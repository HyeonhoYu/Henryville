The news in 3 minutes

AI in special education

CDT, 2025Use is rising
57%

of special education teachers used AI to help with IEPs or 504 plans in 2024-25, up from 39% the year before.

Many districts still lack policies on how AI can be used for these legally required documents.
EdWeek, Oct 2026Time saved
About 45 minutes

One Texas district cut the time to prepare an IEP from hours to an average of about 45 minutes with an AI tool.

Experts say AI can help refine and personalize generic IEP goals when used well, with training and privacy protections.
NPR, May 2026Shortages
45 states

reported special education teacher shortages in 2024-25. Teachers describe AI as freeing time for students.

One teacher described a student who was a non-reader at the start of seventh grade and is now reading.
AdvocatesConcerns
"A Band-Aid"

A CDT report author called AI tools "a Band-Aid" for overworked special education teachers.

Training data reflects mostly neurotypical students, and a 2026 study found AI feedback assumed limited ability for students labeled with disabilities.

Big idea

Three roles for AI in special education

The risk rises as AI moves from helping students to deciding for them.

Access tool

Opens doors

Text-to-speech, speech-to-text, and leveled texts help students take part.

Example: Speech-to-text lets a student with a writing disability draft an essay by talking.
Paperwork assistant

Saves time

Drafting goals, summaries, and family letters for a teacher to revise.

Example: A teacher uses AI to draft IEP goals, then rewrites them using what she knows about the student.
Decision maker

Must stay human

Eligibility, placement, and services are legal decisions about a child's future.

Example: AI should never decide whether a student qualifies for special education.

Click a card for an example. And never paste identifiable student records into a public chatbot.

Takeaways

Three things to remember

Real help for access and workload.

AI can open doors for students and give teachers time back.

Decisions stay human.

Eligibility, placement, and services must be decided by people who know the child.

Protect data and dignity.

Use approved tools, share less, and watch for bias.
